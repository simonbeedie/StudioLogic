Music Video & Advertising: Independent Study

Six weeks of independent study applying the theoretical framework to music video and advertising, ahead of an in-class assessment. Work through one focus per week — watch, read, then apply.

How it works Setup Week 1 Week 2 Week 3 Week 4 Week 5 Week 6 Assessment

How this hub works

Watch → Read → Apply, every week

Each week has a theory focus and a set-text focus. Watch gives you the core explainer video(s) for that week. Read links to the official Eduqas fact sheets for the set texts. Wider comparison brings in an extra text — beyond your set texts — that echoes or challenges what you've just studied, to build the comparative range examiners reward. Apply is your independent task: a short piece of written analysis to complete and bring to the next lesson.

Weeks 1–3 focus on advertising, weeks 4–5 shift to music video (with week 5 linking both), and week 6 is synoptic — pulling every theory and text together ahead of the in-class assessment in week 7.

  • Keep a running glossary of key terms (denotation, connotation, anchorage, myth, binary opposition, preferred reading) — you'll need precise terminology in the exam.

Wider comparison

Dove — "Evolution" (2006)

Watch how this advert exposes and deconstructs the myth Tide's era of advertising relies on — useful contrast for a Barthesian reading.
Apply — independent task

Write one paragraph identifying two signs (image or language) in the Tide advert, explaining their denotation and connotation, and the wider cultural myth Barthes would say the advert constructs (e.g. domesticity, the "ideal" housewife). Then write 2–3 sentences comparing this to how Dove's "Evolution" treats a comparable myth very differently.

Key theorist: Barthes — semiotics, denotation/connotation, myth.

Mrs Fisher — This Girl Can (2015). Note: this video is pitched at GCSE level, so use it for the campaign's binary-opposition logic (capable/incapable, judged/unjudged) rather than as an A-level model answer.

Explain how media language (camera, editing, mise-en-scène, or layout) constructs each side of the opposition, and what ideological message this creates.

Key theorist: Lévi-Strauss — binary oppositions.
Week 3 · Advertising Gender & Disability Representation Theory focus: van Zoonen, bell hooks, Hall ▶
Set texts
Tide SuperHumans

Revisit the Week 1 Tide video and Week 2 SuperHumans video, but this time read them through a representation lens rather than a semiotic or binary-opposition one.

Wider comparison

Gillette — "The Best Men Can Be" (2019)

Search: Gillette "The Best Men Can Be"

A direct rewrite of the domestic gender codes Tide relies on nearly 70 years earlier — useful for tracking how gender representation has shifted (or been contested) over time.

Apply — independent task

Write a short response applying van Zoonen's feminist theory to Tide — what version of womanhood does the advert construct as "normal" or desirable? Then apply bell hooks' concept of intersectionality or Hall's representation theory to SuperHumans — does the advert empower disabled athletes, or does it risk constructing them as "other" through the way it represents difference? Finish with 2–3 sentences linking the two: what do both adverts suggest about whose identity is treated as the default?

Key theorists: van Zoonen — feminist theory, gender. bell hooks — intersectionality, patriarchal oppression. Hall — representation, preferred/oppositional readings.

Billie Eilish — "bury a friend" (2019)

Search: Billie Eilish "bury a friend"

A deliberately constructed, image-driven persona — a useful contrast to the "authenticity" codes Sam Fender's video relies on.

Apply — independent task

Using Gauntlett's "pick and mix" identity theory, write a paragraph on how Underdog or Seventeen Going Under constructs the artist's persona through media language choices (performance style, location, editing, colour). Then use Hall's representation theory to consider whose story is centred and how an audience might read the video's message about class, struggle, or belonging.

Key theorists: Gauntlett — identity, pick and mix. Hall — representation, reception theory.

Apply — independent task

Apply Gilroy's concept of "otherness" to Kiss of the Vampire — how does the poster construct the vampire as culturally, racially, or sexually "other"? Write 2–3 sentences linking your two answers — what do both texts suggest about who is centred and who is "othered" in mainstream media?

Key theorists: Gilroy — postcolonialism, otherness. bell hooks — intersectionality.
Week 6 · Synoptic Audiences & Exam Practice Theory focus: Bandura, Gerbner, Jenkins, Shirky ▶
Set texts
All Section A set texts

No new text this week — the task is to draw everything from Weeks 1–5 together, in line with what the exam expects.

Watch
Audience theory isn't tied to one text, so search each theorist directly rather than following a single link: Mrs Fisher — "audience theory" · The Media Insider — "fandom, participatory culture"
Wider comparison

Fan and remix culture

Look at how fans have responded to, remixed, or reacted to any of your set texts online (YouTube reaction videos, TikTok edits, fan forums). This is your evidence base for Jenkins and Shirky.

Apply — independent task: exam-style extended response

Write a plan (bullet points, not full prose) for the following question: "Compare how audiences might respond differently to two of your set products, using relevant audience theory." Your plan should reference at least two set texts across advertising and music video, and at least two audience theorists (Bandura, Gerbner, Jenkins, Shirky). Bring your plan to the in-class assessment as revision.

Key theorists: Bandura — media effects. Gerbner — cultivation theory. Jenkins — fandom, participatory culture, textual poaching. Shirky — end of audience, prosumers.

In-Class Assessment

Once all six weeks are complete, you'll sit an in-class assessment covering media language, representation, and the theorists applied across advertising and music video.

To prepare:

  • Review your six weekly "Apply" tasks — these are your revision notes.
  • Make sure you can name and briefly define all ten theorists covered.
  • Practise moving between texts confidently: an exam question may ask you to compare an advert and a music video using the same theory.
