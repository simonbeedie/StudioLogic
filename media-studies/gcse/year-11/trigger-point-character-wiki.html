Episode 1 synopsis (Series 2)

Lana Washington has not yet returned to active duty after the traumatic events of series one. While giving a talk to financial-sector security staff about bomb-centred terrorism, an explosion hits a nearby power station. Hass, who has been covering as Senior EXPO in her absence, calls Lana to the scene. She discovers her ex-partner Thom has been promoted and has a new girlfriend, and that the unreliable John Hudson is back on the team. Under a demanding new boss, Commander John Francis, the team follow a clue that may be leading them into a trap.

Key facts for your answer

Trigger Point is produced by HTM Television, a company co-owned by Jed Mercurio (best known for Line of Duty and Bodyguard) and Hat Trick Productions. Mercurio acts as executive producer, overseeing scripts and story decisions, but deliberately does not write episodes himself — he says it is important to keep a professional distance between his own writing career and his producing role. Vicky McClure, the lead actor, is also an executive producer on the show and is closely involved in script notes and production decisions, not just performing.

The show airs on ITV1 (a public service broadcaster funded by advertising) and simultaneously on ITVX, ITV's streaming service — a sign of how UK broadcasters now release "linear" TV and on-demand content together to capture different audience habits. All3Media International distributes the finished series abroad, showing how UK television can generate revenue globally, not just from the domestic broadcast.

The production team describe deliberately building series two to "elevate" what worked in series one rather than changing the format, and they use technical advisors (real bomb disposal expert Joel Snarr and police advisor David Zinzan) to maintain authenticity credibility with audiences.

Media Industries — leading questions

  1. Curran & Seaton argue much of British media is controlled by a small number of powerful figures and companies. How does Jed Mercurio's role across Trigger Point, Line of Duty and Bodyguard support or challenge this idea?
  2. Hesmondhalgh's theory of cultural industries points out that media production is a business as well as an art form. Find two decisions described on this page that were made for creative reasons and two that seem to be commercial or business reasons.
  3. Why might ITV choose to release Trigger Point on both ITV1 and ITVX at the same time, rather than TV only?
  4. What does casting a well-known star like Vicky McClure as both lead actor and executive producer suggest about how ITV tries to reduce financial risk on a new drama?

FILE 01 — EXPO LANA WASHINGTON

Lana Washington

Played by Vicky McClure · also known for Line of Duty, This Is England
Rank/Role: Senior Explosives Officer (EXPO), Metropolitan Police Status: Returning, series 1–presentAlso: Executive Producer

Lana returns to London after six months training Ukrainian bomb disposal teams in Estonia. She is not officially back on duty when the series begins, but is first on the scene when a power station is attacked, so she takes command anyway — a decision that puts her at odds with Hass, who had been covering her role.

Vicky McClure plays Lana as someone who does not fall apart after trauma but channels it into the job; the production deliberately avoided making her a 'quivering mess' after the deaths of her brother and colleague in series one. McClure is also an executive producer on the show, closely involved in script notes as well as acting.

Lana is caught in a complicated position with her ex-partner Thom, who has moved on to a new relationship with DS Helen Morgan while she was away.

Media Language — leading questions

  1. Look at Lana's costume (bomb suit, helmet, 'Explosives Officer' patch). What connotations does this uniform create for the audience before she even speaks?
  2. The actor explains Lana often removes her helmet during a defusal, even though it looks dangerous. Why might the programme makers choose to show this, and what effect does it have on tension for the audience?

Representation — leading questions

  1. Stuart Hall argues the media constructs representations rather than simply reflecting reality. How is Lana's representation as a woman in a dangerous, male-associated job constructed through her actions, dialogue and appearance?
  2. Van Zoonen's feminist theory considers how women are represented in traditionally male roles. Does Trigger Point present Lana's gender as central to her story, incidental to it, or something else? Give evidence.

Audiences — leading questions

  1. Using Gerbner's cultivation theory, what impression might long-term viewers form about the dangers of bomb disposal work or terrorism in the UK from watching a character like Lana?
  2. Why might audiences feel invested in Lana's personal life (her relationship with Thom) as well as her professional heroism? What need might this satisfy for viewers?
FILE 02 — EXPO HASS RAHIM

Hass Rahim

Played by Nabil Elouahabi · also known for Blue Lights, Unforgotten
Rank/Role: Explosives Officer (EXPO), Metropolitan Police Status: Returning, series 1–presentBackground: Former soldier, tours in Afghanistan

Hass has been acting as Senior EXPO while Lana was away and has to hand the role back when she returns. He respects and likes Lana, but there is tension because his position has been unsettled — and because he is genuinely concerned about whether she is ready to return to such dangerous work.

The actor describes Hass as someone who has learned to compartmentalise his emotions after years in the army and on the bomb squad: he stays out of Lana and Thom's relationship entirely and focuses on the job as a form of public service.

Media Language — leading questions

  1. Hass is often shown staying calm and methodical under pressure. What camera techniques or performance choices might a director use to communicate 'calm professionalism' during a bomb disposal scene?

Representation — leading questions

  1. Hass is a British-Arab actor playing a former soldier turned police EXPO. Using bell hooks' idea of intersectionality, consider how his ethnicity, profession and military background might combine to shape how audiences read this character.
  2. How does the professional rivalry between Hass and Lana (over who leads the team) avoid or reinforce stereotypes about workplace conflict between men and women?

Audiences — leading questions

  1. Jenkins writes about fans building attachment to ensemble characters, not just leads. What details from this file might make a loyal viewer feel sympathetic towards Hass rather than seeing him as an obstacle to Lana?
FILE 03 — EXPO DANNY MARKHAM

Danny

Played by Eric Shango · known for Suspicion
Rank/Role: Explosives Officer (EXPO), Metropolitan Police Status: Returning, series 1–presentBackground: Working-class; new to the role in series 1

In series one, Danny was introduced as an anxious, ambitious young Black police officer who came into the job aware of friction between the police and the community he grew up in. By series two he is more secure in his role and his relationships at work, and is now engaged.

Danny is fiercely loyal to Lana but finds it difficult to challenge her decisions because of the strict hierarchy of the police and bomb squad — even when he privately worries she has returned to work too soon.

Media Language — leading questions

  1. Danny's stunts often involve running and physically dangerous action sequences. What effect do fast editing, handheld camera or sound design have on how the audience experiences these scenes?

Representation — leading questions

  1. Stuart Hall's reception theory suggests audiences can read the same representation differently. How might a viewer's own background affect how they interpret Danny's storyline about growing up in a community with police tension?
  2. Gilroy's postcolonial theory considers how Black characters are represented in British media. Compare how Danny is written in series one (anxious, keen to please) with series two (more secure, engaged) — what does this development suggest about representation?

Audiences — leading questions

  1. Why might the producers choose to show Danny's engagement and personal happiness alongside high-stakes action scenes? What audience pleasures does this combination offer?
FILE 04 — DCI THOM YOUNGBLOOD

Thom Youngblood

Played by Mark Stanley · known for Happy Valley
Rank/Role: Detective Chief Inspector, Metropolitan Police Status: Returning, series 1–2Relationships: Ex-partner of Lana; new relationship with Helen

Six months have passed since series one, and Thom has been promoted and started a new relationship with DS Helen Morgan. He is not fully over Lana, but the actor is clear Helen is more than a rebound — the storyline is designed so audiences might sympathise with both women rather than picking an 'enemy'.

Thom is described as career-driven and 'blinkers-on', someone the actor wanted to make more human and sympathetic in series two, even though he makes mistakes in his personal life.

Media Language — leading questions

  1. Thom is shown in both procedural, office-based scenes and high-action sequences (e.g. an attack on a power station). How might mise-en-scene differ between these two types of scene to signal a shift in genre or tone?

Representation — leading questions

  1. How is masculinity represented through Thom's characterisation as a 'career-driven' detective who struggles with relationships? Does this support or challenge typical media representations of male authority figures?

Audiences — leading questions

  1. A love triangle between Thom, Lana and Helen runs through series two. Why might broadcasters include a romantic subplot within an action/crime drama aimed at a broad ITV1 Sunday-night audience?
FILE 05 — DS HELEN MORGAN

Helen Morgan

Played by Natalie Simpson · new to the series in Series 2
Rank/Role: Detective Sergeant, Metropolitan Police Status: New in series 2Relationships: Dating Thom Youngblood

Helen is new to the show in series two: an ambitious, rule-following detective who has risen quickly through the ranks by working hard and following orders — the opposite of Lana's instinctive, sometimes rule-bending approach.

She has been dating Thom for around six months and initially tries hard to be professional and friendly with Lana, though this becomes harder as the series goes on. Helen is more used to office-based investigative work than frontline bomb scenes, so episode one is new and frightening territory for her.

Media Language — leading questions

  1. Helen's introduction contrasts her office-based, procedural world with Lana's frontline bomb disposal world. How might costume, setting or camera work be used to visually establish this contrast?

Representation — leading questions

  1. The actor deliberately wanted audiences to dislike Helen rather than pity her, saying she is often cast as a 'goody'. Using van Zoonen's ideas, how might a female character be written to avoid being purely a sympathetic victim in a love triangle?
  2. Helen is described as a 'perfectionist' trying to please everyone. Is this a stereotype of women in the workplace, a believable individual trait, or both? Justify your answer.

Audiences — leading questions

  1. Reception theory suggests different viewers might side with Helen or with Lana in the love triangle. What details in this file could lead two different audience members to read Helen in opposite ways?
FILE 06 — DR SONYA REEVES

Sonya Reeves

Played by Kerry Godliman · known for After Life
Rank/Role: Data Analyst / Forensics Status: Returning, series 1–presentRelationships: Close friend of Lana

Sonya is a data analyst whose scientific, methodical personality gives Lana a friendship outside the bomb squad hierarchy. Both women work in male-dominated professions, which the actor says bonds them.

Sonya is genuinely pleased to see Lana return from Estonia, and their reunion scene is written to be understated rather than emotional — reflecting two career-focused, practical women who default to talking about work.

Media Language — leading questions

  1. Sonya's role gives her fewer action scenes and more dialogue-based scenes than the EXPO team. How might pacing, lighting or setting differ for her scenes compared with a bomb disposal sequence?

Representation — leading questions

  1. Sonya and Lana are both represented as women in male-dominated professions (data science and bomb disposal). Using van Zoonen's feminist theory, discuss what this friendship suggests about how the programme represents professional women.
  2. The actor jokes that Sonya has become 'desensitised' by her high-pressure job. What does this suggest about how the programme represents coping with trauma at work?

Audiences — leading questions

  1. Why might the writers include a friendship subplot like Lana and Sonya's within a fast-paced thriller? What does it add for the audience alongside the bomb-disposal action?
FILE 07 — COMMANDER JOHN FRANCIS

John Francis

Played by Julian Ovenden · known for Bridgerton
Rank/Role: Commander, Metropolitan Police Status: New in series 2Role: Lana's demanding new boss

Commander Francis is introduced in series two as Lana's new superior, replacing the series one command structure. He represents institutional pressure and authority above the EXPO team.

New senior characters like Francis are often used in returning crime dramas to raise the stakes for the lead character and to introduce fresh conflict without disrupting the established team.

Media Language — leading questions

  1. Senior police characters are often shown in offices, briefing rooms or behind desks rather than in the field. What might this staging communicate about institutional power compared with frontline characters like Lana?

Representation — leading questions

  1. How is institutional authority (the Metropolitan Police as an organisation) represented through a character like Commander Francis, compared with the EXPO team who work directly with the public?

Industries — leading questions

  1. New authority figures are often introduced in a second series. Why might producers make this kind of casting change between series one and two?
FILE 08 — DI AMAR BATRA

Amar Batra

Played by Maanuv Thiara · new to the series in Series 2
Rank/Role: Detective Inspector, Metropolitan Police Status: New in series 2, ongoing

DI Amar Batra is a new detective character introduced in series two, appearing in the wider Metropolitan Police team around the EXPO squad. Less biographical detail about this character was released in the press pack compared with the leads.

This is a good opportunity for independent research: after watching Episode 1, add your own notes on how Batra is represented and what role he plays in the investigation.

Research task

  1. Watch for DI Batra's first appearance in Episode 1. What is his manner, costume and body language when he is introduced?
  2. Compare how much screen time and dialogue a new, less-established character like Batra receives compared with lead characters like Lana or Thom. What might this suggest about how television dramas balance an ensemble cast?
