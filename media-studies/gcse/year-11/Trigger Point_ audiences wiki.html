Who is Trigger Point for?

Producers target audiences, which means they aim a product at a particular group. Your job is to decide who Trigger Point is aimed at and find evidence.

Demographics

Measurable facts about a group, such as age, gender, income, location and social class.

Psychographics

Attitudes, values, interests and lifestyle, such as people who love tense thrillers.

Mass and niche audiences

A mass audience is very large and varied. A niche audience is a smaller group with a specific interest.

Clues to look for

Channel and slot scheduling

Which channel is it on, and when? Sunday night on ITV1 suggests a mainstream adult audience.

Age certificate regulation

The spec lists the set episode as certificate 15, so the target audience is older teenagers and adults.

Stars and creators appeal

Vicky McClure and Jed Mercurio are linked to Line of Duty, which may attract its fans.

Genre appeal

Crime drama and thriller fans expect tension, mystery and twists.

Check your findings after you have researched
  • The Sweeney (1975): only three channels existed, so a hit reached a huge audience at a set time. It was shown at 9pm on weekdays and repeated for years afterwards. Viewers could not pause, rewind or choose when to watch. It was later sold to many countries.
  • Trigger Point Series 1 (2022): broadcast weekly, with no box set until the series ended, which made it appointment-to-view. It averaged 9.5 million viewers per episode.
  • Trigger Point Series 2 (2024): premiered on ITV1 and ITVX together. It averaged 8.1 million viewers and was streamed 30 million times on ITVX.
  • Digital change: on-demand and streaming let viewers choose when and where to watch, on phones and laptops as well as TVs. Viewers can catch up, rewatch and watch on several devices, and social media lets them discuss episodes as they air.
  • Why simulcast? It serves viewers who still watch at a set time on ITV1 and those who prefer on demand. It also gives ITV viewing figures from both.

Why do audiences watch it?

Appeals are the reasons a product attracts an audience. Uses and Gratifications theory says audiences choose media to meet their needs. Your job is to apply it to Trigger Point.

Uses and Gratifications

Diversion gratification

Escape from everyday life and its problems.

Surveillance gratification

Finding out about the world and information.

Personal relationships gratification

Talking about a programme with friends, family or online.

Personal identity gratification

Seeing characters or situations we identify with.

Check your findings after you have researched
  • Diversion: suspense, a ticking-clock plot and unanswered questions let viewers escape into the story and wonder what happens next.
  • Surveillance: viewers learn how bomb disposal and counter-terror policing work. The production used real expert advice, although some details are changed on purpose.
  • Personal relationships: the executive producer says shared viewing creates talking points and theories about what comes next, so viewers discuss it with others.
  • Personal identity: viewers can identify with Lana’s return to work, her competence and the pressure of a changed team, or with other characters’ choices.
  • Other appeals: a star name, high-stakes jeopardy and the reputation of a proven creator.
  • Judgement: diversion is arguably strongest, because the programme is built around suspense, but personal relationships matter too because it is designed as a shared talking point.

Box 4 on your A3 sheet

How do audiences respond?

Audiences do not all respond in the same way. Your job is to apply four theories to Trigger Point and judge how useful each is.

Hall: reception theory

Audiences may take a preferred, negotiated or oppositional reading of a text.

Apply it: Francis’s line “It’s great to see a woman in this job” can be read as warm praise, as well-meant but patronising, or as sexist.

Bandura: social learning

People learn by observing and may copy behaviour they see, especially from role models.

Apply it: do viewers copy Lana’s brave, professional behaviour, or any risky behaviour? Critics say audiences are not passive.

Gerbner: cultivation

Heavy viewers may come to see the world as more dangerous because of what they watch.

Apply it: regular viewers of bomb-threat dramas might overestimate how often terrorism happens. Not every viewer is affected.

Jenkins: fandom and participation

Fans do not just watch. They discuss, create and share, forming communities around a text.

Apply it: viewers swap theories about what will happen, and compare the series with Line of Duty.

Check your findings after you have researched
  • Hall: readings vary because audiences bring different experiences. A viewer who has met workplace sexism may take an oppositional reading of the Francis scene.
  • Bandura: a media effects theory. It suggests viewers might copy what role models do, but criticisms say audiences are active and make their own choices, and it is hard to prove cause and effect.
  • Gerbner: a bomb-threat drama shown regularly might make heavy viewers feel the world is more threatening. Criticism: many other influences affect how people see the world.
  • Jenkins: participation includes online discussion, theories, fan edits and social media posts as episodes air.
  • Regulation and effects: an expert says the show gets the technical details right enough to feel authentic, but not enough to use as a bomb-making guide, which shows the producers think about possible effects.
  • Judgement: audiences are mostly active, because they choose what to watch and interpret it differently, although repeated messages can still shape attitudes.

Model paragraph: how digital technologies change consumption

Digital technologies have changed television consumption from fixed, shared viewing to flexible, on-demand viewing. In 1975, The Sweeney was shown on ITV at a set time, when there were only three channels, so viewers had to watch when it was broadcast and could not rewatch. In contrast, Trigger Point Series 2 premiered on ITV1 and ITVX together, so audiences could watch live or stream it when they chose.

This shows the impact of streaming and convergence: the series averaged 8.1 million viewers and was streamed 30 million times on ITVX. It also changes how audiences discuss television, because they can talk about episodes online as they air. However, some audiences still watch at a set time, which shows that digital change adds choice rather than replacing older habits.

Key terms

Target audience

The specific group a producer aims a product at.

Demographics

Measurable facts about an audience, such as age, gender, income and class.

Psychographics

An audience’s attitudes, values, interests and lifestyle.

Mass audience

A very large and varied audience.

Niche audience

A smaller audience with a specific interest.

Consumption

How, when and where audiences use media.

Linear TV

Programmes shown at a set time on a set channel.

Streaming and on demand

Watching when you choose, online, for example on ITVX.

Appointment-to-view

A programme people make a point of watching at its scheduled time.

Appeal

A feature that attracts an audience, such as a star, genre or storyline.

Uses and Gratifications

Theory that audiences actively choose media to meet needs: diversion, surveillance, personal relationships and personal identity.

Media effects

Ideas about how media influence audience attitudes and behaviour, such as Bandura’s social learning.

Cultivation theory

Gerbner’s idea that heavy viewing shapes how people see the world.

Fandom and participatory culture

Fans taking part in, discussing and extending a text (Jenkins).

Active and passive audience

Active audiences choose and interpret. Passive audiences are seen as simply absorbing messages.

Quick quiz

1. Give one demographic and one psychographic for Trigger Point’s audience.

Demographic: adults, because of the 15 certificate. Psychographic: fans of tense thrillers and of Mercurio’s earlier dramas.

2. Why does ITV need a mass audience?

It is commercially funded, so it earns money from advertising, which depends on large audiences.

3. How many viewers did Series 2 average, and how many ITVX streams?

8.1 million viewers and 30 million streams on ITVX.

4. Give two differences in how audiences watched The Sweeney and Trigger Point.

The Sweeney was shown at a set time on one of three channels and could not be rewatched. Trigger Point is on ITV1 and ITVX, so audiences can stream on demand.

5. Name the four gratifications.

Diversion, surveillance, personal relationships and personal identity.

6. Which theorist says people may copy behaviour they see in the media?

Albert Bandura.

7. What does Gerbner’s cultivation theory say?

Heavy viewers may see the world as more dangerous or threatening because of what they watch.

8. What does Jenkins’s idea of fandom add to our understanding of audiences?

Audiences are active participants who discuss, create and share, not just viewers.
