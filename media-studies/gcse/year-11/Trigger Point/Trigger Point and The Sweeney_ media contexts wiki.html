Box 1 on your A3 sheet

What is context, and why does it matter?

Context is the circumstances a product is made in. Representations reflect the values of the society that produced them, so they change as society changes. Your job is to define context and read what objects mean.

Three types of context

Historical context type

The time period a product was made in, including the events and attitudes of that time.

Social context type

The values, attitudes and issues in society, such as attitudes to gender and work.

Cultural context type

The fashions, entertainment, habits and shared ideas of the time.

Why it matters: representations reflect the values of the time, so they change as society changes. This is Hall’s idea.

Iconography: a car is never just a car

Iconography is the recognisable objects, images and symbols repeatedly associated with a genre or type of character. Barthes gives three steps to read what they mean.

Denotation step 1

What it literally is. The Granada: a 1970s Ford Granada.

Connotation step 2

What it suggests. The Granada: speed, power and pursuit, a car for a tough, action-focused detective.

Myth step 3

A big idea that repeated images make seem normal. The Granada: the “hard man” detective who gets results by any means.

What was the 1970s context behind The Sweeney?

The spec says The Sweeney’s conventions reflect a 1970s interest in blending action with police procedure. Your job is to link four contexts to specific evidence from your ten-minute extract.

The four contexts

Policing 1970s

Before the Police and Criminal Evidence Act (PACE) of 1984. The Flying Squad was investigated for corruption and its real head was jailed in 1977. In The Sweeney, Regan and Carter break rules and use violence to catch criminals.

Gender and work 1970s

Male-dominated workplaces, with equality laws only arriving in the mid-1970s. In The Sweeney, all senior officers and detectives are men, and women are supporting characters, victims or objectified.

Macho culture 1970s

Heavy drinking, smoking and aggression seen as normal in male workplaces. In The Sweeney, Regan and Carter regularly drink, smoke and use violence.

Production 1970s

ITV, three channels, mass audience. The first TV crime drama shot on 16mm film. In The Sweeney: gritty London locations, 1970s fashions and cars, action sequences.

What contexts shape Trigger Point (2024)?

Contexts do not stop in 1975. Your job is to find present-day contexts that shape Trigger Point, with evidence from the episode.

Contexts to explore

Gender and workplace equality 2020s

Lana is the lead and the expert in a dangerous job, and Helen Morgan is a detective sergeant. Yet Francis’s comment and Hudson’s hostility show attitudes have not fully changed.

Ethnic diversity 2020s

The spec describes Trigger Point as a crime drama that incorporates gender and ethnic diversity. Hassan Rahim leads the team in Lana’s absence.

Technology and streaming 2020s

ITV’s ITVX launched as a free ad-funded streamer. Trigger Point premiered on ITV1 and ITVX together, and was streamed 30 million times for Series 2.

Security and modern conflict 2020s

Lana has just returned from training Ukrainian bomb-disposal teams, and an expert notes drones are now central to modern conflict.

Research and authenticity 2020s

The writers spent time with a real squad and experts advised actors. Producers choose where to be authentic and where to dramatise.

  • Technology and streaming: ITVX and simultaneous release reflect a world where audiences expect to watch on demand.

How far has it changed?

A good context answer says what we see (then), how it differs today (now) and why (the reason). The change is real, but partial. Your job is to compare the two dramas and write a context paragraph.

The why is interpretation, so use words such as “arguably”. The pressure-plate bomb comes from the episode climax: check it against your own viewing.

Theory in action

Hall

Representations reflect the values of the time. As society changes, so do the representations we see.

Applied: The Sweeney shows 1970s values. Trigger Point shows today’s.

van Zoonen

Ask who has the power: are women active or passive?

Applied: in The Sweeney women are mostly passive and supporting. In Trigger Point a woman leads.

Lévi-Strauss

Binary oppositions create meaning through pairs of opposites.

Applied: in The Sweeney men are active and tough and women are supportive. Trigger Point blurs the opposition.

Change is real, but partial

Hudson’s hostility and “it’s great to see a woman in this job” show old attitudes have not disappeared. Strong answers show both change and continuity.

Build each paragraph in four steps

  • PointMake a clear point that answers the question.
  • EvidenceGive a specific example from the extract.
  • ExplainSay what it makes the audience think or feel, and why.
  • LinkConnect to a term, a theory, or The Sweeney.

Examiners reward named characters and exact moments from the extract. General points that could apply to any programme earn little credit.

Model paragraph: how crime dramas reflect their historical contexts

The Sweeney reflects its 1970s historical context through its representation of gender. All of the senior officers and detectives are men, and women such as Regan’s girlfriend are supporting characters who are often victims or sexually objectified.

This reflects the male-dominated workplace and gender inequality of the 1970s, and the drinking, smoking and violence reflect the “macho” culture of policing. In contrast, Trigger Point places a woman, Lana, at the centre as an expert, showing how attitudes have changed, although Hudson’s hostility suggests that change is only partial.

Four moves: Point, Evidence, Context link, Contrast. Note where the paragraph explains why, not just describes.

Key terms

Historical context

The time period a product was made in, including the events and attitudes of that time.

Social context

The values, attitudes and issues in society, such as attitudes to gender and work.

Cultural context

The fashions, entertainment, habits and shared ideas of the time.

Iconography

Recognisable objects, images and symbols repeatedly associated with a genre or type of character.

Denotation

What something literally is.

Connotation

What something suggests.

Myth

A big idea that repeated images make seem normal (Barthes).

Flying Squad

The real Metropolitan Police unit, nicknamed ‘the Sweeney’, that tackled armed robbery and violent crime in London.

PACE (1984)

The Police and Criminal Evidence Act, passed to balance police powers with the rights of the public.

Macho culture

A culture in which toughness, drinking and aggression are seen as normal for men.

Patriarchal

Dominated by men, with men holding most of the power.

16mm film

The film format used for The Sweeney, which allowed location filming and gave a gritty, grainy look.

Then, now, why

A pattern for context answers: what we see, how it differs today, and the reason for the change.

Continuity

What stays the same over time. Strong context answers mention change and continuity.

Quick quiz

Say the answer first, then open it to check.

1. Define historical, social and cultural context.

Historical: the time period and events. Social: values, attitudes and issues. Cultural: fashions, entertainment and habits.

2. Why does context matter for representation?

Representations reflect the values of the society that produced them, so they change as society changes.

3. Take the Ford Granada through Barthes’s three steps.

Denotation: a 1970s Ford Granada. Connotation: speed, power and pursuit. Myth: the ‘hard man’ detective who gets results by any means.

4. Name two 1970s contexts behind The Sweeney.

Any two of: pre-PACE policing and Flying Squad corruption, a male-dominated workplace, macho culture, or ITV with three channels and 16mm production.

5. What was special about how The Sweeney was filmed?

It was the first TV crime drama shot on 16mm film, allowing location filming and action sequences.

6. Give one way Trigger Point shows change from The Sweeney.

A woman, Lana, leads as the expert in a dangerous job, whereas women in The Sweeney are mostly supporting characters.

7. Give one way Trigger Point shows that change is only partial.

Hudson’s hostility, or Francis’s comment “it’s great to see a woman in this job”.

8. Which four moves does the model paragraph use?

Point, Evidence, Context link, Contrast.
